# Personal Investment & Finance Guide

## 1. The Foundation: Financial Order of Operations

Before investing, get these in order:

1. **Build a small emergency buffer** (~$1,000) to avoid going into debt for small emergencies
2. **Eliminate high-interest debt** (credit cards, payday loans — anything above ~7%)
3. **Get your employer's 401(k) match** — this is a 50–100% instant return, always take it
4. **Build a full emergency fund** — 3–6 months of expenses in a HYSA
5. **Max tax-advantaged accounts** (Roth IRA, 401(k), HSA)
6. **Invest in a taxable brokerage** for anything beyond that

---

## 2. Emergency Fund

- **Target:** 3 months (stable job, dual income) to 6 months (self-employed, single income, variable pay)
- **Where to keep it:** High-Yield Savings Account (HYSA) — currently 4–5% APY at online banks (Marcus, Ally, SoFi, Discover)
- **Not in:** the stock market — it needs to be available instantly without risk of loss

---

## 3. Debt Strategy

### Pay off first (mathematically)
- Credit cards (15–29% APR)
- Personal loans above ~8%
- Buy Now Pay Later with fees

### Can carry while investing
- Mortgage (deductible interest, typically 3–7%)
- Federal student loans (especially if eligible for forgiveness)
- Auto loans below ~5%

**Avalanche method** (saves the most money): Pay minimums on all, throw extra at the highest-rate debt first.  
**Snowball method** (psychological wins): Pay smallest balance first. Use if you need motivation.

## 4. Tax-Advantaged Accounts (USA)

### 401(k) / 403(b)
| | Traditional | Roth |
|---|---|---|
| Contributions | Pre-tax | Post-tax |
| Growth | Tax-deferred | Tax-free |
| Withdrawals | Taxed as income | Tax-free (after 59½) |
| 2025 limit | $23,500 ($31,000 if 50+) | Same |

**Rule of thumb:** Roth if you expect to be in a higher bracket in retirement. Traditional if you want the tax break now.

### Roth IRA
- 2025 limit: **$7,000/year** ($8,000 if 50+)
- Income phase-out: $150k–$165k (single), $236k–$246k (married filing jointly)
- Backdoor Roth IRA: for high earners above the limit
- Best advantage: contributions (not earnings) can be withdrawn anytime, penalty-free

### HSA (Health Savings Account)
- Only available with a High-Deductible Health Plan (HDHP)
- **Triple tax advantage:** contributions pre-tax, growth tax-free, withdrawals tax-free for medical expenses
- 2025 limit: $4,300 (individual), $8,550 (family)
- At 65, becomes a second Traditional IRA (withdraw for anything, pay income tax)
- Strategy: pay medical expenses out of pocket, save receipts, invest the HSA, reimburse yourself decades later

## 5. Investing Basics

### The core principle
**Time in the market beats timing the market.** The S&P 500 has returned ~10% annually (7% inflation-adjusted) over the long run. Missing just the 10 best days per decade cuts returns roughly in half.

### Asset classes
| Asset | Risk | Expected Return | Liquidity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash / HYSA | Very low | 4–5% (today) | Instant |
| Bonds | Low–medium | 3–5% | High |
| US stocks | Medium–high | 7–10% long-term | High |
| International stocks | Medium–high | Similar to US, diversifies | High |
| Real estate (REITs) | Medium | 7–9% | High (via ETF) |
| Real estate (direct) | Medium | Varies | Very low |
| Crypto | Very high | Speculative | High |

### Diversification
Don't pick individual stocks unless it's money you can afford to lose. The research is clear: most active managers underperform the index over 10+ years.

**The simplest portfolio that works:**
```
US Total Market Index Fund   — 60%
International Index Fund     — 30%
US Bond Index Fund           — 10%
```
Adjust bond % to your age/risk tolerance. Common heuristic: `bonds % = your age` (conservative) or `bonds % = your age - 20` (aggressive).

### Index funds vs. ETFs
- **Index mutual funds** (e.g., VTSAX): buy at end-of-day NAV, no commission, minimum investment may apply
- **ETFs** (e.g., VTI): trade like a stock, can buy fractional shares at many brokers, no minimum
- Both are fine. ETFs are slightly more tax-efficient in taxable accounts.

### What to look for
- **Expense ratio:** below 0.10% is excellent. Avoid anything above 0.50%.
- **Avoid:** actively managed funds, load funds (sales commissions), annuities (usually)

## 7. Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA)

Invest a fixed amount on a fixed schedule regardless of market conditions. This removes emotion and automatically buys more shares when prices are low.

```
Jan: $500 → price $50 → 10 shares
Feb: $500 → price $40 → 12.5 shares  ← buying more at lower price
Mar: $500 → price $60 → 8.3 shares
```

Most brokers support automatic recurring investments. Set it up once and forget it.

## 8. Net Worth Tracking

**Net Worth = Assets − Liabilities**

| Assets | Liabilities |
|---|---|
| Checking/savings | Credit card balances |
| Investment accounts | Student loans |
| 401(k), IRA | Mortgage remaining |
| Home value (market) | Auto loans |
| Car value | Any other debt |

Track monthly. The trend matters more than the number. Tools: spreadsheet, Personal Capital (Empower), Monarch Money, YNAB.

## 9. Insurance (Protect What You Build)

Often overlooked, but one event can wipe out years of savings.

- **Health insurance:** non-negotiable
- **Disability insurance:** your income is your biggest asset — long-term disability (LTD) protects it
- **Term life insurance:** only if others depend on your income; 10–20x your income, 20–30 year term
- **Renter's/homeowner's insurance:** cheap, covers theft, liability, disasters
- **Umbrella policy:** $1M+ liability coverage for ~$150–300/year — worth it once you have assets

Avoid: whole life / universal life insurance (usually a bad investment vehicle sold on commission).

## 10. Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Waiting to start** — time is the most powerful variable. $100/month from age 22 → ~$528k at 65 (7%). From age 32 → ~$243k.
- **Panic selling** — markets crash and recover. Selling locks in losses.
- **Lifestyle inflation** — raises should increase savings rate, not just spending
- **Ignoring fees** — 1% extra fee on $500k costs you ~$100k over 20 years
- **Trying to time the market** — no one does this consistently
- **Neglecting tax efficiency** — put bonds and REITs in tax-advantaged accounts; stocks in taxable
- **No estate basics** — at minimum, set beneficiaries on all accounts

## 11. Simple Rules of Thumb

| Rule | What it means |
|---|---|
| 50/30/20 | 50% needs, 30% wants, 20% savings/debt |
| 4% rule | In retirement, withdraw 4%/year of portfolio for ~30 years |
| 100 minus age | Rough stock % allocation (e.g., age 30 → 70% stocks) |
| 20x rule | Retire when you have 25x your annual expenses saved |
| 1% mortgage | Monthly payment ≈ 1% of home price = rough affordability check |
